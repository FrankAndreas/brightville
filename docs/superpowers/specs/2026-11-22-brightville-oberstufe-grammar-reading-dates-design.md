# Brightville for the Oberstufe, piece 3: grammar (Musterwerkstatt), reading (Bibliothek), dates and times (Kalender)

## Why

Pieces 1 and 2 (`2026-11-22-brightville-oberstufe-vocab-design.md`, PR #67; `2026-11-22-brightville-oberstufe-listening-
design.md`, PR #68) gave the older son (3. Oberstufe, Zurich, LMVZ books) the School as a vocabulary trainer, Schreiben
as dictation and the Reimhaus as listening. The aim stays: **motivate him for English and French**. Piece 3 opens three
more buildings with an Oberstufe side:

- **🧩 Musterwerkstatt: grammar.** Gap sentences by topic.
- **📚 Bibliothek: reading.** Short texts with questions in German.
- **🗓️ Kalender: dates and times.** Saying, reading and writing times and dates.

The parents don't know which grammar his class is on or where he loses points ("build from everything a bit"). So the
grammar covers the usual 3rd-year topics, and **the game finds out where he is weak** and shows it on the parents' page.

## Goals

- Each of the three buildings opens at Oberstufe with its own side, in English or French (never both), and is built and
  merged as **its own PR**, in this order: grammar, reading, dates and times. Each PR is a complete step; the doors of the
  buildings not yet built keep saying "Bald".
- Grammar: 12 topics per language, rounds that favour his weak topics, a record per topic the parents can read.
- Reading: 36 texts per language with a glossary per text and four questions each.
- Dates and times: rounds of mixed tasks generated by rules.

## Non-goals

- Copying the LMVZ books, their units or their wording.
- Grammar he enters himself; texts he brings in.
- Writing free sentences or texts, speaking (piece 5).
- Anything beyond A2 (subjunctive, conditionals beyond the first, reported speech).

## Anti-goals

- **Mixing languages** (as in pieces 1 and 2): a round, its items, choices, glossary and voice are one language plus
  German.
- Anything changing for the Kindergarten child: her Musterwerkstatt, Bibliothek and Kalender stay as they are.
- Grammar explanations that are wrong or vague. The rule line must be short and right.

## Approaches considered

- **Oberstufe sides of the existing buildings (chosen)**, as in pieces 1 and 2: an `oberstufe` entry per building in
  `shared/buildings.json` (with a component of its own and `needs` where a voice is needed), the language screen
  (`LanguagePick`), Stars and visit rules as before, results kept by the server and waiting on the device like the
  vocabulary and listening results.
- Grammar content, three ways:
  - **Mixed (chosen):** the *form* topics (verb forms) are generated from verb tables into sentence frames written by
    hand; the *choice* topics (which tense, some/any, pronouns …) are hand-written sentences.
  - All hand-written (~480 sentences): natural, but much to check and repetitive after a few weeks.
  - All generated from tables: endless and correct forms, but stiff sentences and only form drills.
- One PR for all three (about 25 tasks, one huge review, a tired last part) against **three PRs from one design
  (chosen)**: focused reviews, and he can use the grammar before the rest is done.

## 🧩 Musterwerkstatt at Oberstufe: grammar

### Topics (12 per language)

| English | French |
|---|---|
| present simple vs. present continuous | articles and gender (le/la/l'/les, un/une/des) |
| past simple, with irregular verbs | être, avoir, aller, faire (present) |
| present perfect vs. past simple | -er, -ir and -re verbs (present) |
| will / going to | passé composé with avoir |
| questions and negatives (do/does/did) | passé composé with être (with agreement) |
| comparatives and superlatives | futur proche |
| can / must / have to / should | negation (ne…pas, ne…jamais, ne…plus, ne…rien) |
| some / any, much / many | adjectives: agreement and position |
| adverbs (-ly) | partitive (du/de la/des; "de" after a negation) |
| who / which / that | possessives (mon/ma/mes …) |
| first conditional (if … will) | object pronouns (le/la/les, lui/leur) |
| the passive (simple present and past) | questions (est-ce que, inversion) |

Each topic has a short German name (shown on the shelf), a **rule line** in German (one line, shown after each answer,
e.g. "he/she/it → Verb + -s", "mit être: Partizip angleichen (elle est allée)"), and is either:

- a **form topic**: items made by rules from verb (or noun/adjective) tables put into sentence frames with a slot for
  the subject and the verb, e.g. frame "Hier, {subject} ___ ({verb}) au cinéma." + table → "Hier, elle ___ (aller) au
  cinéma." → "est allée". Form topics: EN past simple; FR être/avoir/aller/faire, -er/-ir/-re verbs, passé composé with
  avoir, passé composé with être, futur proche. Verb tables: the common A2 verbs (about 30 English irregular verbs, about
  30 French verbs), with every form written out in the table (not derived by code), so no form is ever guessed.
- a **choice topic**: about 20 hand-written gap sentences per topic, each with its right answer (and other answers that
  also count, e.g. "isn't" / "is not"), and three wrong choices that are plausible ("some / any / much / many").

Items live in `shared/grammar/en.json` and `fr.json` (topics, rule lines, frames, tables, sentences): first drafts by
Claude at about A2, Swiss context where it fits, for the parents to check.

### A visit

1. He picks the language (`LanguagePick`, no voice needed: the Musterwerkstatt is written only), then a topic or
   **"Gemischt"**.
2. A round is **10 items**. In "Gemischt", topics are drawn weighted towards his weak ones: weight = 1 + (wrong first
   answers of the last 20 in that topic) … the exact weighting is left to the builder, but a topic never tried counts as
   weak, and every topic can appear. Within a topic, items he got wrong last come back first, otherwise at random,
   never the same item twice in a round.
3. **New → choose, known → type**: in a topic he has answered right fewer than 5 times of his last 10 (or never), an
   item shows four choices; otherwise he types the gap. Typed answers are checked like the School's (case, spaces don't
   matter; **accents count** and are marked when only they were off; apostrophe forms like "l'" and "don't"/"do not"
   both accepted when the item lists both). French has the accent buttons.
4. After each answer: right or wrong, the full sentence with the gap filled in and the **rule line**. Wrong: as in the
   School, he types the right answer once to go on, and the item comes back once at the end (no Star).
5. Stars: +1 per item right on the first try, +5 for 10/10 (cap 15).

### The record

Per child and grammar topic (`<lang>:<topic>`): the first answers of the last 20 items (right/wrong, newest last).
Sent and counted as the vocabulary results (a key per round, waiting on the device, counted once). The item ids of the
last wrong answers per topic may be kept with it, so they come back first (left to the builder).

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  🧩 🇫🇷 Passé composé (être)   ⭐⭐○○○○○○○○ │
│  Hier, elle ____ (aller) au cinéma.      │
│  [ est allée                          ]  │
│  é è ê à â ç …              [ Prüfen ]   │
│  Regel: mit être → Partizip angleichen   │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 📚 Bibliothek at Oberstufe: reading

- **Texts:** `shared/reading/en.json` and `fr.json`, by the 12 topics of the word lists, **3 texts per topic** (36 per
  language), first drafts by Claude at about A2, 6–10 sentences, kinds that fit (an e-mail from an exchange student, a
  plan for a school trip, a menu, a notice, a short news item, a diary entry), Swiss context where it fits.
- **A text:** an id, a title (in the language), its sentences, a **glossary** (words or short phrases of the text → German,
  about 8–15 per text, the ones above A2 or easy to misread), and **4 questions** in German, each with four short answers
  in German (or numbers, times, names) and the right one marked; the questions ask for what the text says.
- **A visit:** language (`LanguagePick`), topic or "Gemischt", then **one text**: texts never read first, then those read
  longest ago. The text stays on the screen; the questions below it, one after another, the answers in a new order each
  time. **Tapping a glossary word** shows its German next to it (free, as often as he likes). 🔊 next to a sentence reads
  that sentence in the text's voice (shown only when the device has one; the building itself needs no voice).
- After each answer: right or wrong is shown (wrong: the right one is marked), "Weiter". A question doesn't come back.
- Stars: +2 per question right on the first try, +2 for all four (cap 10).
- **Record:** per child and text (`<lang>:<topic>:<id>`): times read, last day read, questions right last time. Sent and
  counted as before.

## 🗓️ Kalender at Oberstufe: dates and times

All tasks are **generated by rules** (`src/core/…`, no content file), in the language picked.

- **Times** in five-minute steps. English: "It's quarter past eight", "half past", "twenty to nine", "o'clock", and
  "8:15" read as "eight fifteen" also counts; am/pm not asked. French: "Il est huit heures et quart", "et demie", "moins
  le quart", "moins vingt", "midi", "minuit", and the 24-hour form ("seize heures trente") also counts.
- **Dates**: days of the week, months, ordinals (EN first/second/third …, FR premier, then cardinals), dates written in
  words ("the fourteenth of July" / "July 14th" / "le quatorze juillet", "le 14 juillet"), years are not asked.
- **Task kinds**, mixed in a round of **10**:
  1. **Clock → words:** a clock face shows a time; he picks (new) or types (known) how it is said.
  2. **Words → clock:** a time is written, or spoken when the device has a voice; he picks the right clock of four.
  3. **Date in words → date:** a date is written or spoken ("le quatorze juillet"); he picks the date of four (shown as
     14.7.).
  4. **Date → words:** a date (14.7.) is shown; he types it in words ("le 14 juillet" / "July 14th" both count, the
     accepted forms are listed per language).
  5. **Spelling:** a day or month in German (English also ordinals, "der dritte" → "third", as English dates use
     them); he types it in the language (accents count: août, février, décembre). French ordinals are not asked: French
     dates use cardinals ("le trois juillet"), and "le premier" is practised in kind 4.
- **New → choose, known → type** for kinds 1, 4 and 5, as in the grammar (fewer than 5 right of the last 10 → choose,
  from four: other times, other dates in words, other days or months). Kinds 2 and 3 are always picked from four.
- After each answer: the right form shown. Wrong: in a typed task he types it out once; in a picked task the right one
  is marked. Either way it comes back once at the end (no Star).
- Stars: +1 per task right on the first try, +5 for 10/10 (cap 15).
- The existing clock face (`src/buildings/kalender/ClockFace.vue`) shows only full and half hours: it needs minutes in
  five-minute steps for the Oberstufe (the Kindergarten Kalender unchanged).
- **Record:** per child and task kind (`<lang>:<kind>`): the first answers of the last 10. Sent and counted as before.

## The doors

`muster`, `bibliothek` and `kalender` get `"oberstufe"` entries in `shared/buildings.json` **in the PR that builds each**:
maxStars 15 (Musterwerkstatt), 10 (Bibliothek), 15 (Kalender); no `needs` (written, the voice only adds to them). Inside,
a language without a voice can still be picked; 🔊 and spoken tasks simply don't appear (Kalender: task kind 2 and 3 are
then written).

## For the parents

In "Sprachen", per language, more lines as each PR lands:

- Grammar: "Grammatik: N von 12 Themen sicher" and "Schwach: …" naming the weak topics (see below); nothing when none
  is weak.
- Reading: "M von 36 Texten gelesen".
- Dates and times, as for grammar but by task kind: "Uhrzeit und Datum: N von 5 Aufgabenarten sicher" and "Schwach: …".
- "Sicher": 8 or more right of the last 10. "Schwach": tried at least 5 times and fewer than 8 right of the last 10, at
  most three named, those with the fewest right first.

## Calls made in writing this down (Claude's, for the parents to check)

- The 24 grammar topics, the split into form and choice topics, about 20 sentences per choice topic.
- Choose until 5 right of the last 10, then type (grammar and Kalender alike).
- Reading: 3 texts per topic, 4 questions, +2 per question; the glossary per text rather than a dictionary.
- Kalender: five-minute steps, years not asked, both English date orders accepted, the 24-hour form accepted in French.
- "Sicher" at 8 of the last 10; "schwach" at fewer than 8 after at least 5 tries; at most three named.
- Kalender: kinds 1, 4, 5 go from choosing to typing, kinds 2, 3 are always picked; French spelling asks no ordinals;
  the parents see the Kalender by task kind (sicher / schwach), not the score of the last round.
